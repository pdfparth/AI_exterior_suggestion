1Upload

Dark product shell: sticky top bar with a live model chip, a five-step rail on the left, and the working area on the right. The dropzone lifts and glows on hover; a light sweep animates across it while a file is over it.

2Survey

While the quality gate runs, a spinner and indeterminate bar report what is happening. Once the survey returns, each detected surface draws its outline on with a staggered delay, then its fill fades in behind. Colour is shared between the photo and the list, so a row and a shape are obviously the same thing. Hovering either one highlights both.

3Real-world scale

The number that multiplies every cost in the system, so it is surfaced rather than buried: which reference object was used, its assumed size, the model's confidence, and two editable dimensions. Changing either one recalculates the whole estimate immediately.

4Material selection

Grouped by component, each option a swatch card with its rate. Cards lift on hover; selecting one springs a checkmark in and rings the card in brand colour. "Leave as is" excludes a component from both the render and the cost.

5Rendering

The generate button shimmers while working and the status panel says which engine is running. Auto tries Gemini first and composites locally if the API is rate-limited — which is exactly what happened here, and the UI says so plainly rather than passing the cheaper render off as the photorealistic one.

6Before / after comparison

A drag-to-compare slider. On load the "after" image wipes across from 0 to 100%, then eases back to the midpoint so the handle is obviously grabbable. Draggable by pointer, nudges toward the cursor on hover, and arrow-key accessible. Note the roofline and window edges lining up exactly across the seam — the two images stay in register at any split.

7Cost estimate

The total counts up rather than snapping, so a rate change is legible as movement. A split bar shows the material/labour proportion. The rail on the left mirrors the total throughout, so it stays visible while you scroll through materials.

8Rate adjustment

Requirement 5.7 — the user can change rates and see costs recalculate. Kept as a collapsed panel so the estimate view stays clean; the full breakdown of areas, quantities and wastage lives in the PDF report. Here a material rate was changed from 145 to 980 and the total moved from ₹98,782 to ₹4,38,783.

9Excluding a surface

Clicking a row — or the shape on the photo itself — removes that surface from the estimate. The polygon dims, the row greys out, the material picker drops that component, and the cost recalculates. This is requirement 5.2's "adjust or correct" in its simplest usable form.

10Error states

An unusable photo is rejected with a specific reason and practical guidance on retaking it, rather than a generic failure. Toasts carry transient messages; inline notes carry ones you need to keep reading.

11Responsive

Below 1080px the rail moves above the content and the stepper becomes a horizontal scroller. Below 620px the top bar sheds its subtitle, panels tighten, and toasts span the width. No horizontal overflow at any width.

12The whole flow

Every stage in one continuous page — upload, survey, materials, comparison and estimate stay on screen together, so you can scroll back to change a material and watch the total move without losing your place.

Captured from the running app with app.vision and app.redesign mocked — no API key, no network. The redesigned image is genuine output from the local compositor (app/composite.py). Run the real thing with uvicorn app.main:app --port 8000 and open /v2.
